# Structure and pacing

## Shape of a 45 to 70 second video
| Beat | Time | Job |
|---|---|---|
| Hook | 0 to 3 s | A claim, a question or a contradiction. Headline on screen at t=0, presenter present. |
| Stakes | 3 to 10 s | Why the viewer should care in their own life. |
| Mechanism | 10 to 40 s | The explanation, in 3 to 5 beats, each one image-able. Use the metaphor of the world. |
| Turn | 40 to 55 s | The surprising bit, or the fix. |
| Payoff | last 5 s | The line people repeat. Then the CTA if the profile has one. |

## Scenes
- 1.2 to 4.5 s each, average about 2.7 s. A 60 second video has roughly 20 to 24 scenes.
- Cut on a spoken beat: the end of a clause, a stressed word, a breath.
- Presenter windows: shown at the open, hidden when a scene needs the whole frame (a diagram, a big counter, a quote), back for the payoff. Hiding the presenter is a tool; the presenter should be on screen about 55 to 70 percent of the time.
- Alternate density: a busy scene (3 to 5 layers) is followed by a calm one (1 layer).

## Headlines
- 4 words or fewer, one idea. Emphasize one word with `*word*`.
- A headline repeats the scene's key word; it does not transcribe the sentence. Captions do that.

## Layers that earn their place
- `counter` when a number is the point. `compare` for before/after. `list` for 3 steps. `stamp` for a verdict. `circle`/`underline` to point at something on the plate. `quote` for a source. `card` for a screenshot or object.
- Skip decoration. If removing a layer changes nothing, remove it.

## Length
Under 85 seconds. Retention drops after 60 unless each beat escalates. If the script runs long, cut a beat, never speed up the voice past 1.15x.
